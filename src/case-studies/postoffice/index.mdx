## Project context

<Prose>

Postal services in the Nordic region operate through partner retail stores — grocery shops that run a postal counter alongside their own business. Staff at these counters handle parcel intake, shipping sales, and customer service using a dedicated sales application.

Managing staff accounts — adding users, resetting passwords, assigning roles — happened through a separate, older admin portal built into the counter terminal. That portal is what this project replaced.

</Prose>

## The business goal

<Prose>

The old admin portal was reaching end of life, and the business needed a replacement before the cutoff. But this was more than a technical migration — it was a chance to rethink how staff administration worked across the organization.

The goal: **a centralized web portal** that gives authorized business administrators the ability to manage users, roles, and system settings — reducing dependency on IT teams and ensuring consistent operation across Nordic markets. Where the old portal locked every admin task to a single counter terminal, the new SalesApp Admin Portal would work from any device, anywhere.

</Prose>

## The problem

<Prose>

The core business requirement was user administration: create, edit, deactivate, and reactivate staff accounts. Assign roles and permissions. Manage access by country, region, team, or business unit.

The old portal could technically do this — but it was bound to the counter terminal. No remote access, no mobile support. To act as admin, staff had to log the current user out and log back in on the counter machine — admin work and customer service competing for the same desk, the same minute.

For a system supporting **10,000-13,000 employees across locations**, that friction compounded at every store.

</Prose>

<Prose>

Based on business data and insights, I designed the first prototype covering the core user-management flows: **adding store users, resetting passwords, assigning admin roles, updating user details, and deleting users**.

Every concept was pressure-tested against the legacy database's actual limits before going to high fidelity — so what shipped was what could build in a month.

## What testing revealed

<Prose>

I took the prototype to the stores — **site visits and usability testing** with the admins who would actually run the portal. Testing in the real counter environment uncovered problems the business data alone could not surface.

</Prose>

<ImageFigure
  wide
  src={`${IMAGES}/10-testing-annotations.png`}
  alt="The prototype annotated with usability testing findings — thumbs-up for what worked (clean layout, export feature, full-row clicks, color-coded roles) and callouts for what needed to change (role inheritance, auto-populated usernames, hidden unit IDs, default Standard role)."
  caption="The prototype, annotated with real testing feedback — what held up and what the counter changed."
/>

<Prose>

The annotations capture what admins validated and what they pushed back on. Three findings went beyond the prototype and changed the scope entirely:

- **Deleted users could not return.** The old system refused to re-add someone under the same username — turning every seasonal rehire into a workaround. Admins asked to keep deleted users recoverable for about a week before permanent removal.
- **Service accounts mixed with people.** System users sat in the same list as real staff — a problem invisible until admins used the prototype with real data.
- **A paper shadow system.** With no remote access, admins had been copying private staff details into personal notebooks to work from home. They wanted a **physical copy they could rely on** offline or in an emergency. Digitize without meeting that need, and the notebooks come right back.

Two more findings shaped the interaction design: **auto-generated passwords were too complex** to read out at a busy counter, and **mobile access was the #1 request** — the portal became mobile-first, not desktop-first-then-shrunk.

</Prose>

<PullQuote variant="principle">
  You don't kill a workaround by banning it. You kill it by understanding the need underneath it and serving that need better.
</PullQuote>

Testing killed the complex auto-generated passwords. Admins needed to read a password out loud to a new hire across a busy counter — not decipher a random string. The redesign lets admins **set a simple one-time password** on the spot. A security instinct that ignored the counter reality, corrected by the counter.

<DecisionBlock
  index="02"
  title="Role inheritance — one choice, not two"
  media={{
    src: `${IMAGES}/05-role-inheritance.png`,
    alt: 'The add-user form where selecting "Admin" automatically toggles on "Standard User" permissions beneath it.',

>

Selecting **"Admin"** automatically includes **"Standard User"** permissions — because an admin is always also a standard user. During hiring peaks this removed a whole class of configuration errors. Testing confirmed it matched how admins already expected it to behave.

</DecisionBlock>

<DecisionBlock
  index="03"
  title="Secure export — kill the shadow system"
  media={{
    src: `${IMAGES}/04-export-feature.png`,
    alt: 'The portal staff list with a secure Download / Export action — producing a physical-copy export while the live record stays in the cloud as the source of truth.',

>

Site visits revealed the paper notebooks. The fix was not banning them — it was serving the need underneath. A secure **Download / Export** gives admins the physical copy they relied on, while the source of truth stays digital, current, and secure.

</DecisionBlock>

<DecisionBlock
  index="04"
  title="Soft delete with 7-day recovery"
  tradeoff={{

The **7-day soft delete** was not my idea — it came directly from testing. The **Remove** button is high-contrast red to signal the weight, and **Cancel** stays visually neutral, forcing a conscious stop-and-think moment. But the real safeguard is the recovery window behind it.

</DecisionBlock>

The **entire row** is a tap target across every staff table. Paired with **color-coded role chips**, an admin can scan a list and act on the right person without hunting.

## What we shipped knowingly incomplete

<Prose>

Three gaps I would rather name than hide:

- **The 7-day window is still an assumption.** Long enough to catch a mistake or a quick rehire, short enough to purge data — but the correct number is an operations question to pressure-test against real recovery and rehire patterns
- **Export replacing the shadow system is validated in the lab, not yet in the wild.** Testers found it genuinely useful, but whether admins actually stop keeping paper over a full season is a field question to instrument
- **Username reuse stayed bounded by the legacy database.** Soft delete softens the returning-worker problem within the window; fully removing the constraint needed backend change outside a one-month scope

</Prose>

## The takeaway

<Prose>

This project looked like a technical chore — port a legacy portal to the cloud. The design work was refusing to treat it that way.

The prototype gave us the structure. Testing gave us the truth. Admins did not need a nicer version of the old portal — they needed to manage **10,000+ staff accounts from anywhere, on any device**, without the workarounds the old system forced.

Once the goal became **operational freedom** rather than faithful migration, everything followed: mobile-first because that is what admins asked for, a secure export because that is what the notebooks were really for, simple passwords because the counter is not a security lab, and a recoverable delete because the floor told me a careful confirm was not enough.

The decisions I am proudest of here are the ones testing changed.

</Prose>
