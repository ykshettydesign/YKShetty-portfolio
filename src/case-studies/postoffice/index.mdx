## Project context

<Prose>

Postal services in Norway operate through partner retail stores — grocery shops that run a postal counter alongside their own business. Staff at these counters handle parcel intake, shipping sales, and customer service using a dedicated sales application.

Managing staff accounts — adding users, resetting passwords, assigning roles — happened through a separate, older admin portal built into the same counter terminal. That portal is what this project replaced.

</Prose>

## The business goal

<Prose>

The old admin portal was reaching end of life. The business needed a replacement before the cutoff — a mandatory migration on a fixed deadline.

But the migration was also an opportunity. The old portal was physically locked to the counter terminal: no remote access, no mobile support, admin work competing with customer service for the same machine. Store admins had resorted to copying private staff details into **paper notebooks** so they could work from home — sensitive data leaving the secure system entirely.

The goal: **replace the old portal with a cloud-based admin tool** that works from any device — and in doing so, unbind staff management from the counter.

</Prose>

## The problem

<Prose>

The old portal forced five concrete constraints on every admin:

- **No remote access** — everything had to be done at the service desk, so admin work and serving customers competed for the same minute
- **A login tax on every task** — to act as admin, staff had to log the current user out and log back in as admin on the counter machine
- **Deleted users could not return** — remove someone, and the system refused to re-add them under the same username. For shops that rehire seasonal staff, a routine rehire became a workaround
- **Service accounts mixed with people** — system users sat in the same list as real staff, making it hard to find the right person
- **A paper shadow system** — with no remote access, admins copied private staff details into personal notebooks to work from home

</Prose>

The notebooks were the tell: the real failure was not the technology — it was *where* the work was allowed to happen. This was never just a migration. The goal was not "recreate the old portal in a browser" — it was **unbind the work from the counter**.

</Prose>

## How I approached it

<Prose>

I went to the stores. During site visits and face-to-face interviews, one thing became clear — admins were not using paper because they preferred it. They used it because the system was inaccessible anywhere but the counter.

That finding reset the platform priority. **Mobile access was the #1 request** — not a nicer desktop screen. The portal became mobile-first, not desktop-first-then-shrunk.

</Prose>

## The insight that changed the scope

<Prose>

The easy read was: "admins keep paper notebooks, so digitize the list and the notebooks disappear." But field interviews revealed something subtler — admins wanted a **physical copy they could rely on** when away from the counter, offline, or handling an emergency. Digitize without meeting that need, and the notebooks come right back.

So I designed a secure **Download / Export** feature into the portal — meeting the real need (a backup they could hold) while keeping the source of truth digital, current, and secure.

</Prose>

## What testing changed

<Prose>

I put the portal in front of **store admins** — the exact role I had been shadowing — and tested the flows that carried the most risk: landing page, editing a user, resetting a password, and removing a user.

</Prose>

<ComparisonTable
  head={['Flow tested', 'What held up', 'What I changed after']}
  rows={[
    ['Landing page', 'Clean layout, logical navigation, right info surfaced', '—'],
    ['Export', <>Useful for offline records and printing — the shadow-system fix, confirmed by the people who kept the notebooks</>, '—'],
    ['Edit user', 'Full-row click, color-coded chips, admin-implies-standard', <>Default new users to <strong>Standard</strong>; auto-fill usernames; add a last-name column; hide unit ID most staff never need</>],
    ['Reset password', 'Easy to find; confirm-before-reset reassured', <>Auto-generated <strong>complex</strong> passwords changed to <strong>simple, admin-defined</strong> ones</>],
    ['Remove user', <>Confirm is not the primary button — read as a deliberate safeguard</>, <>Added a <strong>7-day soft-delete</strong> recovery window</>],
  ]}
/>

<Prose>

Two changes are worth calling out because they are the reason testing exists.

**The passwords — I was wrong.** I designed auto-generated complex passwords on the logic that stronger is safer. Admins pushed back: too complex to read out or hand to a new hire at a busy counter. They wanted simple, admin-defined passwords they could set and share on the spot. A security instinct that ignored the counter reality, corrected by the counter.

**The recovery window was not my idea.** Testers validated the careful confirmation, then went further — they asked to keep deleted users for about a week before permanent removal. That became the **7-day soft delete**, which quietly solved a second problem: a seasonal worker returning within the window could now be **restored** instead of hitting the "cannot reuse that username" block. One change from the floor closed two gaps.

</Prose>

## AI in the process

<Prose>

A one-month deadline forces sharp choices about where time goes. **Generative AI** brainstormed layout variations for complex staff tables — skipping the blank-page phase entirely. Engineering ran a tight feasibility loop against legacy database limits, shaping what could ship. AI got me out of the gate faster; the field and usability test made sure I solved the right problem.

</Prose>

## What we shipped knowingly incomplete

<Prose>

Three gaps I would rather name than hide:

- **The 7-day window is still an assumption.** Long enough to catch a mistake or a quick rehire, short enough to purge data — but the correct number is an operations question to pressure-test against real recovery and rehire patterns
- **Export replacing the shadow system is validated in the lab, not yet in the wild.** Testers found it genuinely useful, but whether admins actually stop keeping paper over a full season is a field question to instrument
- **Username reuse stayed bounded by the legacy database.** Soft delete softens the returning-worker problem within the window; fully removing the constraint needed backend change outside a one-month scope

</Prose>

## The takeaway

<Prose>

This project looked like a technical chore — port a legacy portal to the cloud. The design work was refusing to treat it that way.

The old portal's real defect was not that it was old — it was that it bound critical work to one physical spot and one login. Once the goal became **operational freedom** rather than faithful migration, everything followed: mobile-first because that is what admins asked for, a secure export because that is what the notebooks were really for, and a recoverable delete because the floor told me a careful confirm was not enough.

The decisions I am proudest of here are the ones testing changed.

</Prose>
